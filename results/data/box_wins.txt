==============================================================================
WHEN DOES THE STILL BOX WIN?
==============================================================================
The inverse question: under what conditions does the STILL BOX win?

The main study established that a creek beats a still box of the same
temperature, always, and explained why the margin is small. This module goes
looking for the exceptions.

==============================================================================
1. The structural obstacle, stated plainly
==============================================================================
  At equal bath temperature, unlimited bath, transverse current:
    U (m/s)   h_free  h_mixed   ratio
       0.00      466      466    1.00
       0.01      466      477    1.02
       0.05      466      645    1.38
       0.35      466     1741    3.73
       1.50      466     4301    9.23

  The blend can never fall below the still-water limit, so the box
  needs a different advantage than the flow field. It has five.

  temperature. Every row below the ice bath goes somewhere no creek
  at 10 C can follow, and the salt-ice bath gets to 1 C in under
  ten minutes while still in a bucket that is not moving at all.

3. LEVER 3: the creek the bottle actually feels
==============================================================================
  Nominal creek 0.35 m/s; still 20 L box reaches 15 C in  16.6 m

    shelter   U felt    ->15C   vs box
       100%    0.350   10.7 m    1.55x
        50%    0.175   11.2 m    1.48x
        30%    0.105   11.7 m    1.42x
        15%    0.052   12.6 m    1.32x
         5%    0.017   14.3 m    1.16x
         0%    0.000   15.6 m    1.07x

  Shelter erodes the creek's margin but never reverses it: at zero
  effective velocity the creek is simply an infinite still bath,
  which still beats a 20 L one. Shelter is a multiplier on the other
  levers, not a lever on its own.

  Where a bottle on the bed actually sits, from the log law:
     depth   U mean  shelter   U felt
      0.20     0.35      79%    0.275
      0.40     0.35      67%    0.233
      0.40     0.80      67%    0.534

4. LEVER 4: a current that opposes the plume
==============================================================================
  A bottle in a downwelling -- the throat of a plunge pool, say --
  meets water moving DOWN past a plume trying to rise.

    U (m/s)  h_forced   h_free  assisting  opposing
      0.000         0      466        466       466
      0.005       183      466        469       463
      0.010       260      466        477       454
      0.020       370      466        507       411
      0.030       456      466        548       251
      0.050       595      466        645       529
      0.100       862      466        879       842
      0.300      1590      466       1593      1587

  Near 0.03 m/s the two mechanisms very nearly cancel, and moving
  water transfers LESS heat than still water. This is the only
  configuration in the whole study where a current is a liability.

6. Time horizon: the gap is not constant
==============================================================================
     elapsed   box 20 L    creek     gap
      30.0 s     37.86C   37.17C   0.70K
      60.0 s     35.92C   34.64C   1.28K
       5.0 m     25.35C   22.02C   3.33K
      15.0 m     15.68C   12.85C   2.84K
      30.0 m     12.25C   10.57C   1.68K
      60.0 m     11.07C   10.06C   1.01K
      2.00 h     10.84C   10.00C   0.84K

  The gap peaks in the middle of the run and then closes again as
  both approach their limits -- but they approach DIFFERENT limits,
  which is the finite-bath effect reasserting itself.

==============================================================================
7. The verdict
==============================================================================
  The box wins whenever you use the one thing it has that a creek
  does not: you choose its contents.

    * Ice water at 0 C beats any 10 C creek outright, at any velocity,
      and 245 g of ice holds a 20 L box there for the whole job.
    * Salt and ice takes it to -21 C and reaches 1 C in under ten
      minutes, with hours of margin before the bottle is in danger.
    * A warm summer creek loses to a bucket of tap water.

  The box loses whenever the comparison is made at equal temperature with
  an unlimited bath, because then the only thing left to compete on is the
  flow field, and a still box has none. Shelter, size and drive all shrink
  the creek's margin, and opposed flow can reverse it locally, but none of
  them is worth as much as six degrees of bath temperature.
