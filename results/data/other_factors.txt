OTHER FACTORS: everything that is not the water outside the bottle
==============================================================================

==============================================================================
1. Internal circulation -- the biggest lever in the problem
==============================================================================
     k_eff  h_inside  U overall  inside share    gain
         1        38         28           85%   1.00x
         2        76         49           74%   1.74x
         7       265        104           45%   3.69x
        15       569        137           28%   4.86x
        30      1137        159           16%   5.63x
       100      3791        178            5%   6.34x

  The free-convection correlation used in the main study gives
  h_inside = 273 W/m2-K for a sealed bottle standing still, which
  is k_eff near 7 -- the row marked above. Perfectly stirred is the
  last row. The difference between them is 1.72x on the
  overall conductance, against the 1.35x that the fastest imaginable
  current buys on the outside film.

  So the creek has a SECOND advantage the main study never counted:
  it does not just flow past the bottle, it knocks it about. A bottle
  wedged still in a creek gets only the film-coefficient gain; one
  rolling along the bed gets this as well, and this is the bigger of
  the two.

2. How full the bottle is
==============================================================================
     fill    volume       UA   heat cap   time const  vs full
     100%      500mL     4.39      2.08kJ/K        7.9min    1.00x
      90%      450mL     3.67      1.88kJ/K        8.5min    0.93x
      75%      375mL     3.12      1.56kJ/K        8.3min    0.95x
      50%      250mL     2.22      1.04kJ/K        7.8min    1.01x
      25%      125mL     1.31      0.52kJ/K        6.6min    1.19x

  Fill level barely moves the time constant, and that is the
  interesting part. Emptying the bottle halfway removes half the
  water to be cooled -- but it also un-wets half the wall, and the
  headspace behind it is nearly a perfect insulator. The two effects
  very nearly cancel: half the heat, half the conductance, same
  time constant. A part-full bottle reaches a given temperature
  sooner only because it started with less heat in it, not because
  it sheds heat any faster.

==============================================================================
3. What is in the bottle
==============================================================================
              contents        Pr         Ra  h_inside   time const  vs water
                 water         5    1.1e+09       615        5.8min     1.00x
     beer / soft drink         8    8.4e+08       541        5.8min     1.00x
                  milk        13    5.4e+08       468        6.0min     1.04x
             olive oil       464    3.2e+07        64        8.0min     1.39x
            thick soup      1520    5.6e+06       114       11.8min     2.05x
                 honey     23000    2.5e+05        49       18.2min     3.16x

  Viscosity decides it. Water, beer and milk all convect freely
  inside and behave alike. Oil, soup and honey do not convect at
  all -- their Rayleigh numbers are too low -- so they cool by pure
  conduction and take many times longer, whatever is going on
  outside. For honey the creek-versus-box question is irrelevant.

==============================================================================
4. Do you need water at all?
==============================================================================
  Bottle at 25 C, air at 20 C, 50% RH (wet-bulb 13.8 C),
  wrapped in a wet cloth:

    wind m/s  sensible  evaporative   total h  vs still water
         0.0       3.3         22.2      25.5           0.05x
         1.0      13.0         88.9     102.0           0.22x
         3.0      23.8        161.9     185.6           0.40x
         8.0      42.1        287.0     329.1           0.71x

  Evaporation carries most of the load -- six or seven times the
  sensible transfer at every wind speed. But even an 8 m/s gale
  reaches only 329 W/m2-K, still 1.4x short of a bucket of
  STILL water and 5x short of a 0.35 m/s creek. Water beats air
  because it is eight hundred times denser, and no amount of latent
  heat closes that gap.

  The wet cloth also cannot go below the wet-bulb temperature
  (13.8 C here), which is above a 10 C creek to begin with. It
  is a good trick in a hot dry place with no water to spare, and a
  poor one anywhere you could simply put the bottle in a stream.
